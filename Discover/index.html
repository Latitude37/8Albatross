01

A pair of distinct residences

Contemporary homes with a natural sense of belonging.

Banksia and Moonah take their names from native coastal species found across the Mornington Peninsula. Like the landscape around them, each home is distinctive, resilient and quietly suited to its setting.

Each residence has its own street frontage, identity and relationship to the land. Construction is underway, with completion anticipated in 2027.

01

8 Albatross Avenue

Banksia

Banksia sits on just under a 1000 square metres and responds to the natural fall of the site rather than resisting it. Pale masonry, deep reveals and warm timber give the home a calm exterior presence, while the main living level opens directly to the pool, landscaped garden and the wider outlook beyond.

8A Albatross Avenue

Moonah

Moonah sits on just over a 1000 square metres and carries a darker, more grounded expression, softened by timber, layered planting and filtered light. Inside, warm stone and deeper timber tones frame living spaces that extend naturally toward the terrace, pool and garden.

A calm sequence from arrival to garden.

Made for daily life

Home feels easier when the plan does the work.

Children can move easily between the living spaces, garden and pool. Family and friends can gather around the kitchen without being separated from what is happening outside. A dedicated study creates a quiet place to work while remaining connected to the rest of the home.

With bedrooms, kitchen, living areas and outdoor spaces brought together across one main level, both homes are also planned with long-term ease in mind.

Now in construction

The long part of the journey is already behind you.

The site has been studied, the design and interiors resolved, and construction is underway. Pools, driveways and landscaping are included, so future owners can step into a considered home without first spending years navigating design decisions, approvals, selections and documentation.

03

Mount Eliza

Close to the coast. Quietly connected to open space.

Mount Eliza offers a rare balance. The village, cafés, restaurants, schools and Moondah Beach remain within easy reach, while at home the outlook is towards paddocks, established trees, the creek below and open sky.

Melbourne’s CBD remains approximately an hour away, yet daily life feels firmly connected to the coast, the landscape and the rhythms of the Peninsula.
